## Cross-cutting strategy (synthesis)

# BookAI Cross-Cutting Product Strategy

## 1. The single most important truth

BookAI is a *demo that looks like a product*. The hard, differentiated 20% — the conversational agent, entity resolution, the 50+ tool library, the morning briefing, the scheduling math — is genuinely built and mostly works. But the boring, trust-bearing 80% that turns a demo into a business it can charge for is either stubbed, silently failing, or wide open: campaigns record `status: "SENT"` and never send (campaigns.ts:84-93, confirmed in code); no-show fees silently `return null` when there's no card; Google two-way sync skips the pull path; subscription tiers are ghost columns no router reads; payment webhooks accept unsigned events; any logged-in user can edit or cancel *any* organization's appointment (appointment.ts:83-86, no `organizationId` in the WHERE clause); the client portal token is unsigned base64 with a code comment that literally says "In production, use JWT or HMAC"; and two clients can book the same slot because conflict-detection and insert aren't in a transaction (engine.ts:22-54). The throughline across all 8 dimensions is the same: **the product advertises autonomy and charges for it, but the system that would make autonomy safe, billable, and trustworthy does not yet exist.** It is one bad week — one cross-tenant data leak, one salon discovering its "sent" campaigns never went out, one double-booked client — away from losing the trust the whole "let the AI run it" thesis depends on. That is the state to fix before anything else.

## 2. The 5-7 highest-leverage moves (ranked)

These are ranked by *compounding impact* — each one unlocks or de-risks several others. I deliberately did not rank "switch to Claude" or "mobile dashboard" at the top; they matter, but they don't compound the way trust/correctness/billing fixes do.

### Move 1 — Close the tenant-isolation + webhook-auth holes, and add an audit middleware. (Effort: M, ~1 week)
**Why it compounds:** This is the single largest blast radius in the codebase and it gates everything else — you cannot sell to a clinic, pass a security questionnaire, or claim "trust" while a `<5-line>` IDOR exposes every tenant's PII and lets anyone forge SMS/Stripe/email events. It also makes the rest of the roadmap safe to build *on top of*.
**What to build, concretely:**
- Add `organizationId: ctx.organizationId` to the WHERE clause of **every** mutation. Verified-missing in: `appointment.update` (appointment.ts:83-86), `appointment.cancel`/`reschedule` (they call scheduling functions that take only an `id` — engine.ts cancel/reschedule never check org), `service.update`/`delete`, `client.update`, `campaign.update`, `location.update`/`delete`, `package.update`, staff override ops. Don't fix these one by one and hope — add a **Prisma `$extends` / client extension that auto-injects `organizationId`** on every query for tenant-scoped models, so a forgotten WHERE clause fails closed.
- Sign the portal token with HMAC-SHA256 + expiry. The pattern already exists in `reschedule-link.ts` (timing-safe compare, exp field) — copy it into `portal.ts:206-212`. ~40 lines.
- Verify webhook signatures: Twilio `X-Twilio-Signature`, Resend `X-Resend-Signature`, and **fail-fast** if `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` is missing instead of processing unsigned events (stripe/route.ts:21-25). Add an `X-Cron-Secret` header check on `/api/cron/*` (currently public in middleware.ts:9).
- Add a tRPC audit-log middleware (userId, orgId, route, mutation type, hash of input, timestamp). You have zero general mutation audit trail today; after an incident you'd be blind.
- Remove `.env` from the repo and make `AUTH_SECRET` required at startup (auth.ts:7 falls back to `"dev-secret-change-in-production"` — committed secrets + forgeable JWTs + IDOR = full account takeover).

### Move 2 — Make booking and money *correct under concurrency*: transactions, idempotency, buffer, timezones. (Effort: M, ~1.5 weeks)
**Why it compounds:** These are the bugs that corrupt data and break the no-show/deposit revenue flows you're about to start charging for. Fixing them is a prerequisite for *both* the monetization work (Move 4) and the scale work (Move 6). They fire in *normal* operation, not edge cases.
**What to build, concretely:**
- Wrap `detectConflicts + appointment.create + client.update` in `prisma.$transaction()` (engine.ts:22-59 — verified zero `$transaction` usage anywhere in the codebase). Same for `rescheduleAppointment`. On Postgres, add a partial unique constraint on `(staffMemberId, startTime)` for non-group services as a DB-level backstop, and use `Serializable` isolation for the booking path.
- Add `idempotencyKey` to the AI `chat` mutation and the `/api/booking` endpoint; store it on `AiActionLog` and short-circuit retries. Without it, a timed-out retry double-books (this is the most common support ticket you'll get at volume).
- Use `totalDuration = durationMinutes + bufferMinutes` in `availability.ts:86` *and* `engine.ts:20` (verified: `bufferMinutes` is computed then never used — staff get zero prep time, the appointment-runs-late complaint).
- Fix the timezone family as **one coordinated change** with a single `date-fns-tz` pattern (`toZonedTime` → set hours → `fromZonedTime`): availability slot construction (availability.ts:90-91 builds dates in *browser-local* time while existing appointments are in org TZ — a systematic hot-path mismatch), waitlist 24h window, recurrence DST. And fix the hard crash at `waitlist.ts:72` (`new Date('14:30')` → epoch-1970 appointment).

### Move 3 — Ship the stubbed features that are advertised as working (campaigns, waitlist notify, two-way sync). (Effort: M, ~1-1.5 weeks total)
**Why it compounds:** These are pure trust debt — the UI says they work, the logs say "SENT," and they don't. The day a salon discovers it, you've lost them and the referral. They're also cheap relative to impact, and two of them (campaigns, no-show prevention) are the *revenue-recovery* story that should headline your repositioning (Move 7).
**What to build, concretely:**
- Campaigns: branch on `campaign.channel` and call `sendEmail/sendSms/sendWhatsApp` *before* writing the execution row; set status `PENDING → SENT/FAILED` based on the actual send result (campaigns.ts:84). Fix the BIRTHDAY eligibility bug noted in verification (no `birthday` field → it would blast the entire client base).
- Waitlist: add `sendWaitlistNotification()` and call it when `checkWaitlistOnCancellation()` flips an entry to `NOTIFIED`. This is the entire point of a waitlist.
- Google two-way sync: implement the `pullFromGoogle` event-creation path so externally-created calendar events block availability (google-sync.ts:275 explicitly punts on this). Today, if an owner blocks lunch in Google Calendar, BookAI still sells that slot — which directly contradicts "the AI runs your schedule."
- Stop logging stub sends as `status: "SENT"` (email.ts:64-69) — it poisons the audit trail. Add a startup health check that *throws* in production if a configured provider's keys are missing.

### Move 4 — Make money actually flow and be metered: tier enforcement, card-on-file, Stripe Connect onboarding, usage tracking. (Effort: M-L, ~2-3 weeks)
**Why it compounds:** Right now the business model is fiction. Free orgs get everything (no router reads `subscription.tier`), upgrades don't change the tier because there's no `customer.subscription.updated` webhook handler, the revenue-share orgs have no Connect account so 100% of fees stay with the platform and they see $0, and you have no idea what any org *costs* you. Until this is real you can't price, can't prove unit economics, and can't tell a profitable customer from a loss-making one.
**What to build, concretely:**
- `tieredMiddleware` in trpc.ts that reads `subscription.tier` and gates SMS/WhatsApp, multi-staff, analytics, and AI volume. Wire the Stripe subscription webhooks (`customer.subscription.updated`, `invoice.payment_succeeded`) to actually flip `tier` — today it only changes via a manual mutation.
- Mandatory **Setup Intent (card-on-file)** at booking when `service.depositRequired`/`bookingPage.requirePayment` is set, *before* confirming the appointment. Add `checkPaymentMethod()` and call it before `chargeNoShowFee` (payments.ts:296 silently returns null today). Also: `chargeNoShowFee` doesn't verify the appointment is actually `NO_SHOW` and `Math.round(amount * pct/100)` floors to $0 on cheap tickets — add a minimum-fee floor and a status guard.
- Stripe Connect OAuth onboarding flow + a Transfers dashboard + `transfer.failed` handling. Without this the "free + 4%" model literally cannot pay anyone.
- A `UsageLog` table (AI tokens, SMS, email, with cost) written on every provider call, plus per-org daily spend caps and a circuit breaker in the AI loop. This is *also* the cost-guardrail that prevents one runaway campaign or one chatty client from burning $100/day (agent.ts has retries but no per-org quota).

### Move 5 — Migrate SQLite → Postgres with pooling and the right composite indexes. (Effort: XL, ~2-3 weeks)
**Why it compounds:** It's the unlock for transactions/`SELECT FOR UPDATE` (Move 2 is far stronger on Postgres), real distributed rate limiting (Move 1), RLS as a *second* safety net behind your app-level org checks (Move 1), and any concurrency at all. SQLite's single-writer lock means every org's reads and writes contend for one lock; combined with 5s global polling (Move 6) it caps you at a handful of concurrent operators. Do the cheap transactional fixes first (they work on SQLite), then migrate.
**What to build, concretely:** dual-write → cut reads → stop writing → decommission. Add `@@index([organizationId, status, startTime])` (calendar view) and `@@index([staffMemberId, startTime, status])` (availability) — the current indexes cover `organizationId+startTime` but not status. Configure pgBouncer (transaction mode) and an explicit Prisma `connectionLimit`. Enable RLS policies so a missed app-level org check fails at the DB.

### Move 6 — Replace 5s global polling with a unified "needs-attention" inbox backed by SSE. (Effort: M, ~1.5 weeks)
**Why it compounds:** This single move solves three separate dimensions at once: (a) the **operator UX** fragmentation — today the owner checks 4 routes (Messages, Request Centre, Appointments, Dashboard) for what needs attention; (b) the **architecture** load — 20+ tRPC queries × every tab × every 5s, with `refetchIntervalInBackground: true`, is the dominant DB-load multiplier and the cause of stale-data double-bookings; (c) the **AI approval** confusion (Messages vs Request Centre show the same approvals). Build `/dashboard/inbox` consolidating approvals, escalations, no-show-risk appointments, pending confirmations, and new bookings, sorted by urgency with batch actions — and feed it (plus Calendar) via org-scoped SSE instead of polling. Disable `refetchIntervalInBackground` immediately as a one-line stopgap.

### Move 7 — Pick the salon vertical and reposition around revenue capture, not "AI." (Effort: M for messaging + L for vertical features)
**Why it matters:** "Salons/medspas/clinics/fitness" means optimized for none, while Fresha (now shipping dynamic AI reassignment), Mindbody, and Boulevard each own one. And Square can give an AI assistant to 100M payment-enabled SMBs at zero CAC — so feature parity is *not* the moat. Reframe the landing page from "Run your business by talking to AI" to a concrete outcome claim: **"Charge for no-shows. Fill cancellations. Book more clients."** Each line backed by a number once Moves 3-4 make them real. This is cheap (copy) but only credible *after* campaigns send, no-show fees charge, and waitlist notifies — which is why it's last in sequence even though it's a top-tier GTM lever.

*(Lower-tier but worth noting: lower agent temperature to ~0.2 for booking calls and add conversation windowing + a per-call token budget — agent.ts:97/111. Cheap, improves determinism and caps cost. And the mobile/tablet dashboard gap — fixed 256px sidebar, AI chat `hidden lg:block` — matters for the mobile-owner positioning but doesn't compound, so it slots after the trust/money work.)*

## 4. The moat question: is "autonomous conversational scheduling" defensible?

**Honest answer: not as currently framed, and not on its own.** The conversational layer — reply-to-anything multi-channel routing + a function-calling agent — is genuinely differentiated *today*, but it is a 6-12 month moat at best. Fresha already ships dynamic AI reassignment; Square is attaching an AI assistant to a 100M-SMB payment base at zero acquisition cost; Calendly/Square have SMS workflows. "The AI books for you" will be table stakes within a year, and the underlying model (GPT-4o-mini, swappable) is not proprietary. Worse, BookAI's version of "autonomous" is *partly fictional right now* — campaigns don't send, two-way sync doesn't pull, no-show fees don't reliably charge — so it can't even claim the autonomy it has.

**What would actually make it defensible** (in increasing durability):
1. **Be the only one whose autonomy is real and end-to-end.** Campaigns send, calendar syncs both ways, no-show fees auto-charge, waitlist auto-fills, approvals resolve cleanly. Incumbents over-claim and under-ship; shipping *exactly what you claim* is a near-term wedge. This is Moves 2-4.
2. **Vertical depth as accumulating advantage.** A generic CRUD agent doesn't understand stylist-to-station affinity, color-mixing blocking a shared resource, or esthetician rotation. Encode salon resource/constraint modeling into `context-builder.ts` and the agent prompt so the AI schedules around *physical reality*, not just time slots. That domain logic is expensive to copy and gets better with usage.
3. **Proprietary outcome data → a learned, improving system.** The no-show predictor is hand-tuned weights today. Replace it with a model trained on *your* completed-vs-no-show labels per org. Every booking makes the prediction (and the auto-prevention: deposit-on-risk, confirmation cadence) better. This is the only piece that compounds with scale and that a new entrant cannot clone by reading your marketing page.
4. **Integration + ecosystem lock-in.** Wire to POS/inventory/payroll so the agent can say "you have 3 days of dye left, cap color bookings." Plus a Stripe-Connect-backed revenue dashboard the owner checks daily. Switching cost, not feature count, is what keeps a $9/mo customer for two years.

The defensible product is *not* "conversational scheduling." It's "the system that reliably recovers revenue (no-shows, cancellations, rebooks) for one vertical, gets measurably smarter at it with every appointment, and is wired into the tools that vertical already runs on."

## 5. Suggested sequencing

**Next 2 weeks — Stop the bleeding (trust + correctness, cheap, no infra change):**
- Move 1 in full (IDOR org-scoping via Prisma extension, HMAC portal token, webhook signature verification, cron secret, remove `.env`, audit middleware). This is the highest-severity, lowest-effort cluster in the whole report.
- Move 2's SQLite-compatible parts: `$transaction` on create/reschedule, buffer-minutes fix, the coordinated timezone fix, the waitlist 1970 crash, idempotency keys. (`Serializable`/`FOR UPDATE` waits for Postgres.)
- One-line stopgaps: disable `refetchIntervalInBackground`; lower agent temperature to 0.2 for booking; stop logging stub sends as `SENT`; add a startup health check that throws on missing prod provider keys.

**Next 2 months — Make it real and billable:**
- Move 3 (ship campaigns/waitlist-notify/two-way-pull; fix BIRTHDAY eligibility).
- Move 4 (tier middleware + upgrade webhooks; card-on-file Setup Intent; Connect onboarding + transfers dashboard; UsageLog + per-org spend caps + circuit breaker).
- Move 6 (unified `/dashboard/inbox` + SSE for Calendar/Inbox; merge the Messages/Request-Centre approval confusion).
- Begin Move 5 (Postgres dual-write) — overlaps because transactions and rate-limiting get materially stronger on it.
- Lower-cost UX: extend onboarding to include Stripe Connect + Google + messaging setup; deposit/payment enforcement and SMS reschedule links on the public booking page.

**Next 6 months — Build the actual moat:**
- Finish Move 5 (cut over to Postgres + pgBouncer + RLS + composite indexes); run staged load tests and set SLOs.
- Move 7 vertical commitment: salon-specific resource/constraint modeling in the agent; salon-tuned features (post-service review automation, smart cancellation fill).
- Replace the rule-based no-show predictor with a learned, per-org model fed by accumulated outcome data, and wire it to *automatic* prevention (deposit-on-risk, escalated confirmation cadence).
- Reposition GTM around proven, measurable revenue recovery with real customer case studies; introduce vertical-aware tiered pricing.
- Mobile/tablet dashboard (responsive sidebar, AI chat bottom-sheet), proactive morning-briefing push, and queue/job infrastructure (QStash is in `.env.example` but unused).

## 6. Blind spots the 8 dimensions did not cover

A great product team would also examine these — several are launch-gating for the medspas/clinics segment specifically:

- **HIPAA / regulated-data compliance.** The product explicitly targets *medspas and clinics*, yet there's no BAA story, no PHI handling policy, no encryption-at-rest requirement, and — critically — client notes/intake-form data flow straight into an OpenAI prompt with no DPA-aware redaction. This is not a nice-to-have; it's a *legal gate* for half the stated market, and it argues for the "salons first, clinics later" sequencing in Move 7.
- **Analytics & product instrumentation.** There is no event analytics (activation, time-to-first-booking, feature adoption, funnel drop-off). You cannot run the pricing A/B tests or prove the unit-economics story without it. Pairs naturally with the UsageLog work in Move 4 but is distinct (product telemetry vs cost telemetry).
- **Observability / alerting.** No Sentry, no structured logging, no on-call alerting. Revenue-impacting silent failures (a Stripe charge that fails, a sync that breaks, a cron that half-completes) are invisible today. This is a prerequisite for trusting *any* of the autonomy features in production.
- **Churn / retention loops.** The reports cover acquisition and pricing but not *keeping* customers: no dunning/retry on failed subscription payments (`subscription.status` is hardcoded `active`), no renewal reminders, no win-back for the business *itself* (ironic, given it sells churn-fighting tooling), no health-score/at-risk-account surfacing.
- **Data export / portability & account lifecycle.** No verified export (clients, appointments, payment history) and no account-deletion/data-retention flow. This is both a GDPR/CCPA obligation and a trust signal ("I'm not locked in"), and its absence raises switching *fear* rather than switching cost.
- **Accessibility & i18n.** No mention of ARIA labels on icon-only controls, keyboard navigation, or WCAG; no localization despite a clientele (salon staff, clients) that is frequently multilingual. The booking widget and client portal especially are public surfaces where accessibility is table stakes and arguably a legal exposure.
- **Deliverability & messaging compliance.** SMS A2P 10DLC registration, STOP/HELP keyword handling, opt-in/consent tracking, and email SPF/DKIM/DMARC. Since the entire product is "clients reply to messages," carrier filtering or a TCPA complaint is an existential operational risk that none of the 8 dimensions addressed.
- **Disaster recovery / backups.** With SQLite as the production store (a single file), there is no described backup, point-in-time recovery, or RPO/RTO. One corrupted file or bad deploy could lose every tenant's bookings. The Postgres migration (Move 5) should explicitly include automated backups as part of the cutover, not as an afterthought.

### Product & UX completeness
BookAI has a coherent core product with clean foundational UX, but suffers from fragmentation across critical operator workflows and lacks essential Polish for mobile use. The 19-item sidebar nav is sprawling but well-organized; however, the core deficiency is the absence of a unified "needs my attention" inbox that brings together approvals, escalations, at-risk appointments, and new bookings scattered across 4 routes (Messages, Request Centre, Appointments, Dashboard). The public booking widget is functional but lacks deposit/payment enforcement at booking, SMS reschedule links, and mobile optimization. The client portal is complete but underutilized due to booking flow friction. Mobile responsiveness is completely absent: the 256px sidebar is fixed, AI chat panel hides on lg: breakpoint only, and no touch-friendly alternatives exist for tablet/mobile owners. Onboarding is polished but bypasses critical setup (no payment method setup, no calendar sync walkthrough). The biggest product risk is that multi-user collisions from 5s polling will degrade UX at scale, and the lack of a single "needs attention" destination leaves owners checking 4 places for urgent actions.

### AI agent quality — the core moat
BookAI's agent architecture is operationally sound but strategically constrained. The engine relies on GPT-4o-mini with a 5-iteration tool-call cap, deterministic handlers, and entity resolution that works for well-formed queries but fails silently on ambiguous or malicious input. Core strengths: solid entity resolver with disambiguation, comprehensive 50+ function library, validation gating via Zod, function call audit trails. Critical gaps: (1) model choice is under-powered for complex multi-turn reasoning (4o-mini is fast + cheap but loses context on long conversations); (2) unbounded conversation history (stored as JSON in SQLite, no truncation) will hallucinate or miss context at scale; (3) resolution search uses case-insensitive substring matching, enabling cross-org or cross-client booking errors under specific conditions; (4) no-show predictor is rule-based heuristic, not learned; (5) prompt injection possible via client notes/tags/messages without sanitization; (6) temperature 0.7 is too high for deterministic booking — should be &lt;0.3. The 5-iteration cap truncates legitimate workflows (book appointment → check availability → resolve staff → reschedule → confirm = 5 calls, leaving no margin for errors). This is the moat, but it's fragile: competitors with Claude 3.5 Sonnet, streaming tool-use, and learned rankers would outmaneuver decisively.

**Top product moves:**
- Switch to Claude 3.5 Sonnet for multi-turn operator chat (keep 4o-mini for single-turn client bookings) to unlock reasoning depth and reduce hallucinations—this is the highest-leverage moat-builder. A salon owner using Claude will finish their 30-minute schedule in 5 minutes instead of 15, and reschedules will Just Work. Competitors will struggle.
- Implement conversation windowing (keep last 12 turns + summary) and token budgeting (3k-token budget per turn) to prevent context overflow and token cost explosions. Pair this with a learned no-show predictor (simple XGBoost on historical data) to make no-show scoring accurate enough that salons trust the AI to auto-send strong reminders. This compound feature—reliable context + accurate risk scoring—is table-stakes for $9/month SaaS retention.
- Increase the iteration cap from 5 to 8 and implement smart error recovery (reserve 2 iterations for retries, timeout per iteration) so multi-step workflows don't silently fail. Test with 'book John for 2-hour session, any time, flexible' and 'reschedule all Tuesday clients to Wednesday' — these are real workflows that break today.
- Add prompt injection defenses (sanitize names, notes, tags before injecting into system prompt) and implement per-function temperature tuning (0.15 for booking, 0.7 for coaching). These prevent silent data corruption and ensure deterministic bookings—the foundation of customer trust.

### Architecture & scalability
BookAI runs on SQLite in production (packages/db/.env = "file:./dev.db"), despite docker-compose provisioning Postgres + Redis. This is the architecture's single biggest scalability risk: SQLite enforces writer-exclusive locking, making multi-tenant concurrent writes (bookings, calendar syncs, approvals) serializable but slow. The 5-second polling interval (providers.tsx:19) broadcasts every query from every browser tab to all users every 5s, without deduplication. Crons have no idempotency guards—overlapping runs can double-process reminders, calendar syncs, and campaigns. No transaction safety on appointment+client creation (booking/route.ts:203-222 does unguarded sequential writes). Rate limiting exists but is in-memory, non-persistent across restarts, and trivially bypassed by restarting. Missing: connection pooling, database indexes on calendar queries, idempotency keys, background job queues, realtime sync, and load tests. The hardening plan exists but is deferred—Phase 1 (Postgres migration) is the critical blocker for any multi-tenant scale.

**Top product moves:**
- Migrate from SQLite to PostgreSQL with connection pooling (Phase 1 hardening). This unblocks 6 of the 7 deferred hardening phases and is the table-stakes move for multi-tenant scale. Without it, 50 concurrent users is infeasible.
- Replace 5-second global polling with targeted SSE/websocket for Calendar and Appointments screens (Phase 2). Reduces database load 80%, improves UX to realtime, eliminates 'stale data' conflicts that cause duplicate bookings and operator friction.
- Add transaction safety to appointment creation (wrap client.create + appointment.create in $transaction) and idempotency keys to AI-driven mutations. Prevents orphaned appointments and duplicate bookings from retries—the two most common data integrity issues.
- Add distributed cron locks + per-org AI usage quotas. Prevents duplicate reminders/campaigns and runaway costs from one org's misconfigured campaign overwhelming all orgs. Foundational for ops reliability.

### Security & multi-tenant isolation
BookAI has critical multi-tenant isolation vulnerabilities that directly expose customer data. VERIFIED: appointment.update (packages/api/src/routers/appointment.ts:83-86) and service.update/delete (service.ts:42-54) lack organizationId checks in their WHERE clauses, allowing any authenticated user to modify/delete any org's records via IDOR. Portal token encoding is unencrypted base64 (portal.ts:210-212), guessable by enumeration. Email/Twilio webhooks are completely unverified—any attacker can inject fake messages. The Stripe webhook conditionally skips signature verification if STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET is missing (stripe/route.ts:20-25). Auth secrets are committed to repo with fallback to "dev-secret" (auth.ts:7). A hardcoded "base64url token = org:client" in portal.ts:193 means attackers who know two tenant IDs can forge access. Rate limiting is in-memory, in-process (non-distributed), and easily overwhelmed. These aren't edge cases—every mutation that accepts an ID without verifying organizationId is a cross-tenant attack vector.

**Top product moves:**
- FIX IDOR vulnerabilities immediately: Audit all 22 tRPC routers and add organizationId checks to EVERY mutation WHERE clause (appointment.update, service.update/delete, client.update, staff schedule operations). This is the largest blast radius bug in the codebase.
- Replace unencrypted portal token with cryptographic signature: Implement HMAC-SHA256 signing for portal tokens (like reschedule-link.ts does). This prevents 1-click takeover of client portals and is a <100-line fix with massive security uplift.
- Implement webhook signature verification for email + Twilio + Stripe: Add Resend signature check (X-Resend-Signature), Twilio signature validation (X-Twilio-Signature), and fail-fast on missing Stripe secret. This prevents message spoofing attacks and takes ~200 lines total.
- Kill the hardcoded auth secrets: Remove .env from repo, make secrets required (fail startup), and document secret rotation. This is a quick win (2 PRs: git history cleanup + startup check) that closes JWT forgery risk.

### Competitive positioning, moat & GTM
BookAI's core positioning — "reply-to-anything autonomous AI booking" — is differentiated but facing serious competitive response from incumbents (Fresha's dynamic reassignment already shipping) and horizontal players adding AI (Calendly, Square). The moat is real but narrowing: BookAI's actual advantage is multi-channel reply routing (email/SMS/WhatsApp) + function-calling agent logic, but this is table-stakes within 12 months. The bigger risk: BookAI's unit economics are broken for salons/medspas (target vertical). At 4% revenue share, SMS+email costs ($0.008 per SMS + $0.0013 per email) + OpenAI ($5/1M tokens) + Stripe fees (2.9%+$0.30) consume 30-40% of that margin before running any product. Subscription tier ($9/mo) doesn't scale to profitability unless vertically focused. GTM risk is severe: no clear vertical wedge (positioning says "salons/medspas/clinics/fitness" — pick ONE), no network effect, and customers have weak incentive to switch from Boulevard/Fresha (2-year incumbency, staff trained, integrations live) unless BookAI solves a specific, documented pain they experience daily. The campaign system is stubbed (records but never sends), so revenue-share model is untested. The critical path to defensibility: ship genuine end-to-end autonomy (campaigns send, Google Calendar syncs bidirectionally, no-show fees charge), own one vertical at 3-5x the conversion rate of incumbents, and prove a unit-economics story where revenue per customer grows (not just margin per booking).
